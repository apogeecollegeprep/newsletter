01 - Guidance from Apogee

Leveling the Playing Field

By Terri

Did you know that Apogee College Prep is deeply committed to supporting dedicated students every single year?

While we operate as a private consultancy, our business was founded on a core belief: expert college guidance should be accessible, not exclusive. That is why every coach on our team is dedicated to taking on scholarship recipients each application season.

Our impact spans both our local communities and the national stage. We proudly offer coaching scholarships to talented students in Atlanta through local Community-Based Organizations, and nationally, we partner with the College Essay Guy's Matchlighters program to guide motivated scholars from across the United States.

We also help exceptional students navigate rigorous pathways such as the QuestBridge National College Match, The Posse Foundation Scholarship, and the Jack Kent Cooke College Scholarship Program.

Why this work matters

Seeing these incredible young adults cross the finish line and thrive is at the heart of why we do this. We are immensely proud to see our scholarship graduates gain admission and matriculate to institutions such as Hamilton College, Agnes Scott College, and many more.

A college education can change the economic forecast for future generations, and at Apogee, we truly make a difference.

To our current and past families: Thank you. By partnering with Apogee, you are not just investing in your own student's success; you are helping us level the playing field, invest in our communities, and open doors for exceptional students who need it most.

03 - Whole-student support

A Note to Seniors: Navigating Application Season

By Danielle Kay

Hello Seniors:

Take a deep breath, unclench your jaw, and drop your shoulders. You're right in the thick of college application madness and juggling application deadlines with your academic obligations and extracurriculars. If you're feeling excited one minute and completely overwhelmed the next, that's 100% normal. Here are some tips for navigating this turbulent time.

  • Aim for done, not perfect. Overediting will cause every word to look like a typo and will cause you to second-guess yourself along the way. Set a timer, write, and step away when the timer ends.
  • Protect your free time. Your brain cannot function on 24/7 stress. Put "zero application talk" blocks on your calendar, go outside, watch a mindless show, stare into the abyss, or talk with friends about literally anything else. Your essays will still be there when you're refreshed and ready.
  • Give yourself permission to feel all the feelings. If the Common App glitches or a teacher hasn't submitted a recommendation yet, it's okay to freak out or cry. Get it out, grab a snack, and tackle one tiny task next.
  • Block out the social media noise. People are only posting their acceptances; they aren't posting the rejections or the anxiety. Stay focused on your own journey, and if everyone else's news is causing you stress, log off for a while.
  • Set boundaries around portals and college talk. Constantly refreshing portals won't make decision day arrive any faster. Check your email and student portals once a day, then move on. Tell the adults in your life clearly whether you need someone to proofread or whether you simply need some space.

Remember, you are not on this journey alone. The Apogee College Prep team is here to support you. Whether you need essay advice, help organizing your applications, technical support, or a pep talk, please reach out.

This marathon won't last forever. Right now, it may feel like a high-stakes race where the finish line keeps moving, but you will cross it. You are awesome right now, and you are so much more than a GPA or an admission decision letter.

04 - The month in admissions

September News to Know

These are the 10 September developments selected by Apogee for their concrete relevance to undergraduate applicants and families. Follow the link beneath each summary to read the original reporting.

Admissions Policy

NC College Connect Offers Direct Admission to 67,500 North Carolina Seniors

UNC System - September 15

North Carolina's NC College Connect program extended direct-admission offers for the 2027-28 academic year to more than 67,500 public high school seniors, about 64% of the state's senior class, without requiring a traditional application. Eligible students began senior year with at least a 2.8 weighted GPA and are enrolled in a fourth-level math course; personalized offers identify participating four-year institutions where each student already has a seat.

Legacy Preferences Have Fallen Sharply but Remain Common at Highly Selective Colleges

Inside Higher Ed - September 17

A Class Action analysis of more than 1,700 four-year colleges found that the share considering legacy status fell from about half in 2015 to 23% in 2024. Legacy preferences remain concentrated at selective institutions: 57% of colleges admitting 25% or fewer applicants still reported using them, while public colleges rarely use the preference.

Georgia Tech Explains How Intended Major Fits Into Application Review

Georgia Tech Admission Blog - September 1

Georgia Tech says first-year applicants are not admitted to a specific major or college; admissions readers instead evaluate institutional fit across the application. The university nevertheless expects applicants to show a considered connection to their selected major through coursework, activities, and the way they explain how Georgia Tech would extend their interests.

WVU Launches Direct-Admission Pilot for Seniors in Two Counties

WVU Today - September 11

West Virginia University launched WVU Express, a pilot offering direct admission to qualifying high school seniors in Monongalia and Kanawha counties for the Morgantown campus. Eligible students avoid the traditional application and fee; school counselors convey offers after WVU evaluates academic achievement, and students then complete authorization and enrollment steps.

Application Trends

Common App Data Shows 57% of Applicants Report Major Home Responsibilities or Hardships

Inside Higher Ed - September 3

New Common App data show that more than 870,000 of 1.5 million first-year applicants in the 2025-26 cycle, about 57%, reported at least one substantial household responsibility or difficult life circumstance. The standardized question was added for all applicants so admissions readers can interpret grades and activities in context.

University of Maryland Adds Three Undergraduate Majors and Three Minors

Maryland Today - September 9

The University of Maryland introduced undergraduate majors in Global Culture and Thought, Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence, and Environmental and Resource Economics for fall 2026. New minors cover Artificial Intelligence in Architecture, American Sign Language, and Chesapeake Bay watersheds and water resources; the AI-in-architecture minor is limited to architecture majors.

College Financial Health

New Financial Health Tracker Flags Warning Signs at Hundreds of Four-Year Colleges

The Hechinger Report - September 8

The Hechinger Report launched a College Financial Health Tracker using a decade of federal data, accreditation decisions, and reporting on layoffs and program cuts. Among roughly 1,900 primarily bachelor's-granting colleges, about 1,100 experienced repeated enrollment declines, approximately 1,500 had lower net tuition revenue, and nearly 440 ended three of five fiscal years with deficits. The warning signs can translate into fewer majors, courses, advisers, and student services even when a college does not close.

Financial Aid

Wellesley Raises Tuition-Free Income Threshold to $200,000

Wellesley College - September 9

Beginning in fall 2027, Wellesley College will charge no tuition to U.S. students from families earning up to $200,000 a year with typical assets, raising its current threshold from $150,000. Wellesley says it will continue need-blind admission for U.S. applicants and meet the full calculated need of all admitted students.

Marist Caps Total Price at $32,000 for Eligible New York Families

Inside Higher Ed - September 15

Marist University launched the Empire State Promise, which will cap tuition, housing, and dining at $32,000 per year for eligible New York residents from families earning under $200,000. The announcement describes an income threshold, but families should verify any additional eligibility conditions directly with Marist.

Carleton Adopts No-Loan Financial Aid Beginning Fall 2027

Inside Higher Ed - September 15

Beginning in fall 2027, Carleton College will replace federal and institutional loans in need-based aid packages with grants, scholarships, and student employment after a family pays its calculated contribution. The policy will cover incoming students and returning first-years, sophomores, and juniors.
