01 - Guidance from Apogee

Leveling the Playing Field

By Terri Jacobson, Principal Coach

Did you know that Apogee College Prep is deeply committed to supporting dedicated under-resourced students every single year?

While we operate as a private consultancy, our business was founded on a core belief: expert college guidance should be accessible, not exclusive. That is why every coach on our team is dedicated to taking on reduced-fee and pro-bono recipients each application season.

Our impact spans both our local communities and the national stage. We proudly offer coaching scholarships to talented students in the Southeast and throughout the country. Locally, we work through Community-Based Organizations, and nationally, we partner with the College Essay Guy's Matchlighters program to guide motivated scholars from across the United States.

Why this work matters

Seeing these incredible young adults cross the finish line and thrive is at the heart of why we do this. We are immensely proud to see our in-need graduates gain admission and matriculate to institutions such as Hamilton College, Agnes Scott College, The University of Edinburgh, Howard University School of Law, Fordham University, Boston University, Colgate University, the University of Delaware, and Batten University (formerly Virginia Wesleyan University), where one of our students is a Batten Honors recipient—and more.

A college education can change the economic forecast for future generations, and at Apogee, we truly make a difference.

To our current and past families: Thank you. By partnering with Apogee, you are not just investing in your own student's success; you are helping us level the playing field, invest in our communities, and open doors for exceptional students who need it most.

03 - Whole-student support

A Note to Seniors: Navigating Application Season

By Danielle Kay, Essay Coach

Hello Seniors:

Take a deep breath, unclench your jaw, and drop your shoulders. You're right in the thick of college application madness and juggling application deadlines with your academic obligations and extracurriculars. If you're feeling excited one minute and completely overwhelmed the next, that's 100% normal. Here are some tips for navigating this turbulent time.

  • Aim for done, not perfect. Overediting will cause every word to look like a typo and will cause you to second-guess yourself along the way. Set a timer, write, and step away when the timer ends.
  • Protect your free time. Your brain cannot function on 24/7 stress. Put "zero application talk" blocks on your calendar, go outside, watch a mindless show, stare into the abyss, or talk with friends about literally anything else. Your essays will still be there when you're refreshed and ready.
  • Give yourself permission to feel all the feelings. If the Common App glitches or a teacher hasn't submitted a recommendation yet, it's okay to freak out or cry. Get it out, grab a snack, and tackle one tiny task next.
  • Block out the social media noise. People are only posting their acceptances; they aren't posting the rejections or the anxiety. Stay focused on your own journey, and if everyone else's news is causing you stress, log off for a while.
  • Set boundaries around portals and college talk. Constantly refreshing portals won't make decision day arrive any faster. Check your email and student portals once a day, then move on. Tell the adults in your life clearly whether you need someone to proofread or whether you simply need some space.

Remember, you are not on this journey alone. The Apogee College Prep team is here to support you. Whether you need essay advice, help organizing your applications, technical support, or a pep talk, please reach out.

This marathon won't last forever. Right now, it may feel like a high-stakes race where the finish line keeps moving, but you will cross it. You are awesome right now, and you are so much more than a GPA or an admission decision letter.

September News to Know

These are the 9 September developments selected by Apogee for their concrete relevance to undergraduate applicants and families. Follow the link beneath each summary to read the original reporting.

Admissions Policy

Georgia Tech Explains How Intended Major Fits Into Application Review

Georgia Tech Admission Blog - September 1

Georgia Tech says first-year applicants are not admitted to a specific major or college; admissions readers instead evaluate institutional fit across the application. The university nevertheless expects applicants to show a considered connection to their selected major through coursework, activities, and the way they explain how Georgia Tech would extend their interests. The guidance does not require a fixed career plan and points students toward career and major-research tools, including My Next Move, the Occupational Outlook Handbook, and O*NET.

Application Trends

Common App Data Shows 57% of Applicants Report Major Home Responsibilities or Hardships

Inside Higher Ed - September 3

New Common App data show that more than 870,000 of 1.5 million first-year applicants in the 2025-26 cycle—about 57%—reported at least one substantial household responsibility or difficult life circumstance. Thirty percent spent at least four hours per week caring for younger siblings or other household members; 19% provided family transportation, 16% assisted with appointments or paperwork, 10% had commutes longer than an hour, 8% lacked reliable internet, and about 5% reported homelessness. The standardized question was added for all applicants last year so admissions readers can interpret grades and activities in context.

Three-Year Bachelor's Programs Expand to 119 Offerings Across 26 States

Poets & Quants for Undergrads - September 9

Reduced-credit bachelor's degrees are expanding: a RAND analysis counted 119 three-year programs across colleges in 26 states as of May 2026. Some require as few as 90 credits instead of the roughly 120 typical of a four-year degree, potentially eliminating a year of tuition and living expenses and allowing graduates to enter the workforce sooner. The programs remain new, however, and students may encounter uncertainty about whether employers or graduate schools will view the shorter credential as equivalent to a traditional bachelor's degree.

University of Maryland Adds Three Undergraduate Majors and Three Minors

Maryland Today - September 9

The University of Maryland introduced three undergraduate majors and three undergraduate minors for fall 2026, alongside two graduate offerings. The new bachelor's programs are Global Culture and Thought, Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence, and Environmental and Resource Economics; students can begin the human-centered AI curriculum this fall and choose among seven specializations. New minors cover Artificial Intelligence in Architecture, American Sign Language, and Chesapeake Bay watersheds and water resources.

HBCU Application Growth Pushes Spelman's Admit Rate Down to 19%

Inside Higher Ed - September 17

Several HBCUs are reporting sustained growth in applications and enrollment even as many other colleges face recruitment pressure. Spelman College in Atlanta said rising application volume pushed its admission rate from 51% to 19% over five years while the college kept enrollment relatively stable; Morgan State's enrollment rose from 7,151 in 2019 to 12,477 in 2026, and North Carolina A&T reached 15,713 students this fall. The growth has also created capacity pressures involving housing, financial aid, registration, advising, and student services.

SAT Scores Rise for the Class of 2026 as Participation Slips

Higher Ed Dive - September 30

The College Board's 2026 SAT report shows a mean total score of 1,045 for the high school class of 2026, up 16 points from the class of 2025, with gains in both test sections and across all reported demographic groups. About 42% of test takers met both college- and career-readiness benchmarks. More than 1.95 million students took the SAT, but participation declined 2.5%, the first decrease since the pandemic-affected class of 2021.

College Financial Health

Greenway Institute Plans Fall 2027 Opening With a Low-Cost, Work-Based Engineering Model

The Hechinger Report - September 1

The Greenway Institute, a new engineering college being developed on the former Vermont College of Fine Arts campus in Montpelier, tentatively plans to open in fall 2027. Its model would place students on campus for two years of faculty-supervised project work and then in paid workplace placements for two years, with annual tuition expected to be $25,000 or less. Greenway has raised $4.5 million toward a $30 million goal and expects to need about 370 students by 2031 to break even; because it cannot apply for accreditation until it opens, its first students would not initially be eligible for federal grants or loans.

New Financial Health Tracker Flags Warning Signs at Hundreds of Four-Year Colleges

The Hechinger Report - September 8

The Hechinger Report launched a College Financial Health Tracker using a decade of federal data, accreditation decisions, and reporting on layoffs and program cuts. Among roughly 1,900 primarily bachelor's-granting colleges, about 1,100 experienced enrollment declines in three of the five years from 2019 through 2024, approximately 1,500 had lower net tuition revenue, roughly 960 institutions employed 75,600 fewer instructional staff, and nearly 440 ended three of those five fiscal years with deficits. The report illustrates how financial pressure can mean fewer programs, courses, advisers, and student services even when a college does not close.

Financial Aid

Georgia Tech Remains Princeton Review's Top Value Public College

Georgia Tech News - September 29

Georgia Tech placed first for the fourth consecutive year on The Princeton Review's Top 50 Best Value Public Colleges list, earning a return-on-investment score of 95 out of 99. The university says inflation-adjusted attendance costs for Georgia residents are nearly $4,400 lower than in 2019, a 27% reduction, and that it has expanded need-based scholarships through fundraising. The ranking also cited Georgia Tech's career placement and reported a median graduate salary of $126,000 after 10 years.
